summary: Frequent sneezing, runny or blocked nose, itchy eyes and postnasal drip triggered by allergens such as dust, pollen or pet dander.
intro: >-
  Allergic rhinitis is one of the most common allergy-related concerns we see
  at Abhaya Allergy Center. It happens when the lining of the nose reacts to
  airborne substances the body treats as harmful, producing sneezing,
  congestion and irritation that can affect sleep, concentration and daily
  comfort.
symptoms:
  - Repeated bouts of sneezing, often first thing in the morning
  - Runny nose or, conversely, a persistently blocked nose
  - Itching in the nose, eyes, throat or ears
  - Watery or red eyes
  - Postnasal drip and throat clearing
  - Reduced sense of smell and disturbed sleep
triggers:
  - House dust and dust mites
  - Pollen from grasses, trees and weeds (seasonal)
  - Pet dander and feathers
  - Mould spores and damp environments
  - Strong smells, smoke and sudden changes in temperature
whoAffected: >-
  Allergic rhinitis can begin in childhood or adulthood. It is often seen
  alongside asthma, eczema or sinus problems, and there is frequently a family
  history of allergy.
whenToSeekHelp: >-
  Consider a consultation if symptoms are frequent, disturb your sleep,
  interfere with work or study, do not settle with simple measures, or occur
  together with breathing difficulty or recurrent sinus infections.
consultationApproach: >-
  A consultation begins by understanding your symptom pattern, likely triggers,
  medical history and how the condition affects your daily life. This helps
  build a picture of your individual sensitivity so that care can be tailored
  and, where relevant, further evaluation suggested.
faq:
  - question: Is allergic rhinitis the same as a common cold?
    answer: >-
      They can feel similar, but a cold is caused by a virus and usually clears
      within a week or two, while allergic rhinitis tends to recur or persist
      and is linked to specific triggers rather than infection.
  - question: Can allergic rhinitis affect children?
    answer: >-
      Yes. In children it can show up as constant sneezing, a blocked nose,
      mouth breathing, poor sleep or difficulty concentrating. A consultation
      can help understand the pattern.
  - question: Do symptoms change with the seasons?
    answer: >-
      For some people symptoms are year-round (often dust related), while for
      others they flare in particular seasons when certain pollens are in the
      air.

Allergic rhinitis develops when the immune system reacts to normally harmless
airborne particles. Recognising your personal triggers is an important step
toward managing symptoms and protecting your comfort and sleep.

If nasal symptoms are affecting your everyday life, a consultation can help you
understand what may be driving them and how care can be approached for your
situation.
