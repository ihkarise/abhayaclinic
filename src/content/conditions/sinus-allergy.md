summary: Persistent nasal congestion, facial pressure, headaches and recurrent sinus discomfort linked to allergic inflammation.
intro: >-
  Sinus-related allergy symptoms occur when the tissues lining the sinuses
  become inflamed and swollen, often as a continuation of ongoing nasal
  allergy. This can lead to blockage, facial pressure and recurring discomfort.
symptoms:
  - Blocked nose and difficulty breathing through the nose
  - Pressure or heaviness around the cheeks, forehead or eyes
  - Recurrent headaches
  - Thick nasal discharge or postnasal drip
  - Reduced sense of smell and taste
  - Discomfort that worsens on bending forward
triggers:
  - Ongoing allergic rhinitis and airborne allergens
  - Dust, smoke and air pollution
  - Damp environments and mould
  - Frequent upper respiratory infections
whoAffected: >-
  People with long-standing nasal allergy, frequent colds, or a tendency toward
  recurrent sinus symptoms are more likely to experience sinus-related
  discomfort.
whenToSeekHelp: >-
  Seek advice if sinus pressure and congestion keep returning, last a long
  time, are associated with frequent headaches, or affect your sleep and daily
  activities.
consultationApproach: >-
  A consultation looks at how often symptoms occur, what seems to bring them on,
  and your overall history, so that care can be planned for your individual
  pattern and further evaluation arranged where appropriate.
faq:
  - question: How is sinus allergy different from a sinus infection?
    answer: >-
      Allergic sinus symptoms are driven by inflammation from allergens, while
      an infection involves bacteria or viruses. They can overlap, which is why
      a proper assessment is helpful when symptoms keep returning.

Recurrent sinus congestion and facial pressure are often connected to
underlying nasal allergy. Understanding this link can help you and your doctor
plan care that addresses the pattern rather than only the flare-ups.
