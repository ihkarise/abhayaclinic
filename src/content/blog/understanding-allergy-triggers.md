Many people live with sneezing, a blocked nose, itchy skin or recurring hives
without realising these symptoms often trace back to specific, identifiable
triggers. Understanding what sets off your symptoms is one of the most useful
first steps in managing any allergy.

## Why triggers matter

An allergy is the body reacting to something it treats as harmful, even when it
is normally harmless. The reaction produces the symptoms you feel — congestion,
itching, wheezing or a skin flare. When you know which triggers affect you, it
becomes easier to reduce exposure and to have a more informed conversation with
your doctor.

## Common triggers to notice

- **House dust and dust mites** — often behind year-round nasal symptoms.
- **Pollen** — grasses, trees and weeds, usually seasonal.
- **Pet dander** — from cats, dogs and other furry animals.
- **Mould and damp** — common in humid indoor spaces.
- **Smoke, strong smells and sudden temperature changes** — frequent irritants.

## Keeping a simple symptom diary

For two to three weeks, note when your symptoms appear, where you were, and what
you were doing. Patterns often emerge — symptoms every morning may point to dust
in bedding, while symptoms outdoors in a particular season may point to pollen.

## When to seek a consultation

Consider a consultation if your symptoms are frequent, disturb your sleep,
interfere with work or study, or occur alongside breathing difficulty. A
consultation can help you understand your individual pattern and how care can be
approached.
